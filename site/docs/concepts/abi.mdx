---
title: The C ABI
description: One header, opaque handles, explicit ownership, and a version every SDK checks before anything else.
---

`gosslens.h` is the whole public surface. It's C99 and includes nothing beyond
`stdint.h`, `stddef.h` and `stdbool.h`. Every symbol starts with `goss_`.

## Handles are opaque

`goss_engine` and `goss_session` are pointers to types you never see inside,
and they're the only two handles that cross. Everything else lives behind the
session, which is why there's no third lifetime for a binding to get wrong.

The only structures that cross are plain-data descriptors, and their layouts
are static-asserted in the engine's tests and documented field by field in the
header.

## Ownership is in the name

```c
goss_status goss_session_create(goss_engine *engine, const goss_session_config *config, goss_session **out_session);
void        goss_session_destroy(goss_session *session);
```

Every `create` has a `destroy` and every `acquire` has a `release`. Anything
that can fail returns `goss_status`. There's no errno, and no exception crosses
the boundary, because every C++ shim catches at its own edge and turns it into
a status.

## Vectors cross by pointer

A position, a quaternion, a matrix or a colour goes across as a pointer to a
fixed-size array, never spread out into separate float parameters.

That's an ABI hazard, not a style preference. Past the eighth float argument
the x86_64 calling convention starts spilling to the stack, and a caller and
callee built by different toolchains can disagree about that layout. Passing
the aggregate keeps every scalar in a register and marshals the same way
everywhere.

## Versioned, and checked first

```c
uint32_t v = goss_abi_version();  // high 16 bits major, low 16 minor
```

The minor comes from the operation list plus the field count of every struct
that crosses, so adding a field to a record moves the number on its own and
nobody has to remember to bump it. An SDK compares the major and refuses to run
if it doesn't match.

There's no compatibility shim, and you don't want one: continuing past a major
mismatch means reading the wrong bytes at the wrong offsets, and that usually
produces numbers that look believable.
